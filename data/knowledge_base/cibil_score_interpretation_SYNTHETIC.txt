SYNTHETIC CIBIL SCORE INTERPRETATION GUIDE (ILLUSTRATIVE)

This is a synthetic internal reference document for underwriting staff,
written for a capstone project demo. It summarizes widely known, public
knowledge about how CIBIL-style credit scores are generally interpreted
by Indian lenders. It is not an official TransUnion CIBIL publication.

SCORE BANDS

300-549 (Poor): Indicates a history of missed payments, defaults, or
very limited credit history combined with risk flags. Applications in
this band are typically declined or require substantial compensating
factors (large down payment, strong co-applicant, secured collateral).

550-649 (Fair): Below-average repayment history. Loans may be approved
at a higher interest rate to compensate for elevated risk, or routed to
manual underwriting for a closer look at the applicant's recent (last
6-12 months) payment behavior rather than their full history.

650-749 (Good): Generally reliable repayment history with occasional,
minor delays. Most lenders will approve standard loan products at
standard rates for applicants in this band, subject to income and
debt-to-income checks.

750-900 (Excellent): Strong, consistent repayment history. Applicants in
this band typically qualify for the lowest interest rates a lender
offers and the highest loan amounts relative to income.

NO SCORE / THIN FILE

An applicant with no CIBIL score (because they have never taken formal
credit) is NOT the same as an applicant with a poor score. A "thin file"
simply means there isn't enough credit bureau history to compute a score.
For first-time borrowers, this platform's Document Intelligence module
(bank statement analysis) and Fraud Detection checks are used as
supplementary signals, consistent with the fair-treatment principle in
the lending guidelines document in this knowledge base.

HOW THIS PLATFORM'S CREDIT ENGINE RELATES TO CIBIL BANDS

This platform's Module 1 (credit_engine.py) does not consume a raw CIBIL
score directly — the underlying training dataset (Kaggle's "Give Me Some
Credit") instead uses payment-history-derived features (times 30-59/60-89/
90+ days late, revolving credit utilization, debt ratio) that are
conceptually similar to what feeds into a real CIBIL score calculation,
and produces its own LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH risk band and a calibrated default
probability. The two scoring systems are directionally comparable but
numerically distinct — a real deployment would typically use both the
model's own score AND a bureau-pulled CIBIL score as independent checks.
