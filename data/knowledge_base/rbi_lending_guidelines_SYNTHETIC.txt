SYNTHETIC LENDING GUIDELINES SUMMARY (ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT THE REAL RBI MASTER CIRCULAR)

This document is a synthetic, illustrative summary written for a capstone
project demo. It paraphrases widely known, general-knowledge principles of
responsible retail lending in India. It is NOT an excerpt from, and should
NOT be cited as, any actual Reserve Bank of India circular or direction.
See data/knowledge_base/README.md for how to add the real document.

SECTION 1 — FAIR PRACTICES IN LENDING

Lenders are expected to assess a borrower's repayment capacity before
sanctioning any credit facility, rather than relying solely on collateral
value. The loan application form should clearly indicate the information
required, and the fees payable. All key terms and conditions of a loan —
interest rate, processing fees, prepayment charges, and the method of
interest application (reducing balance vs flat) — should be communicated
to the borrower in a document they can keep, before the loan is disbursed.

SECTION 2 — DEBT-TO-INCOME CONSIDERATIONS

A commonly used rule of thumb across retail lenders is that a borrower's
total monthly debt obligations (existing EMIs plus the proposed new EMI)
should not exceed roughly 50-55% of verified monthly income for salaried
applicants, and a somewhat lower threshold for self-employed applicants
given income variability. Applicants above this threshold are typically
routed to manual underwriting review rather than being auto-declined
outright, since compensating factors (e.g. a co-applicant's income,
substantial savings) can offset a high ratio.

SECTION 3 — KYC AND IDENTITY VERIFICATION

Every loan applicant must be identified through valid Know Your Customer
(KYC) documents — proof of identity, proof of address, and (for income-
based lending decisions) proof of income such as salary slips, Form 16,
or bank statements. Document Intelligence modules that extract income
and obligations from an uploaded bank statement are a supplementary signal
and do not replace formal KYC verification.

SECTION 4 — GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL

Lenders are expected to maintain a grievance redressal mechanism and
display the name and contact details of the Grievance Redressal Officer
at every branch and on the website. An applicant who is declined a loan
is entitled to know the principal reason for the decision upon request —
this is one reason this platform's Decision Engine (Module 4) always
produces a plain-English explanation alongside every verdict, rather than
a bare APPROVE/DECLINE flag.

SECTION 5 — INTEREST RATE DISCLOSURE

The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) — the effective annual cost of the loan
including processing fees — should be disclosed to the borrower, not just
the nominal interest rate, so that offers from different lenders can be
compared on a like-for-like basis.

SECTION 6 — FAIR TREATMENT OF VULNERABLE BORROWERS

Special care is expected when assessing applications from first-time
borrowers, senior citizens, or applicants with limited formal credit
history — a thin credit file should not, by itself, be treated the same
as a poor credit history. Alternative data points (such as bank statement
analysis, as implemented in this platform's Module 3) are an accepted way
to supplement a thin credit file.
